**1\. Executive Summary & Abstract**

Structural integrity within biological systems cannot exist without electrons to establish and maintain chemical bonds. In severe sepsis, systemic metabolic derangements drive localized tissue environments into profound states of electron and charge imbalance.

This report presents a formalized biophysical model demonstrating how the influx of positive charges ($H^+$) alters the algebraic net charge of solid frameworks (bone mineral matrices and soft-tissue protein scaffolds). The mathematical negation of stabilizing charge states leads to spontaneous bond dissociation, triggering the phase transition of solid, organized physiological architecture into amorphous liquid states—a universal pathomechanical definition of a lytic lesion.

**2\. Mathematical and Physics Foundations of the Model**

**2.1 The Algebraic Tracking of Charge and Electron Transfer**

To align biological mechanisms with strict mathematical and physical laws, we model structural stability based on net elementary charge states ($z$). Let a neutral system possess an initial charge of zero ($z \= 0$).

* **Electron Gain (Reduction)**:  
  $$\\Delta z \= \-1 \\implies z\_{\\text{final}} \= \-1$$ 

* **Electron Loss (Oxidation)**:  
  $$\\Delta z \= \+1 \\implies z\_{\\text{final}} \= \+1$$ 

In physical chemistry, the standard notation for a proton is $H^+$, representing a naked hydrogen nucleus missing its electron ($1s^0$). The accumulation of these protons acts mathematically as a profound localized deficit of negative charge carriers ($e^-$).

When evaluating the equilibrium of a molecular structure, introducing an external positive charge carrier ($H^+$) algebraically neutralizes an existing localized negative charge carrier:

$$(+1) \+ (-1) \= 0$$ 

By driving the net regional charge toward zero or altering its sign, the Coulombic attractive forces that bind human structures together are abolished.

**2.2 Thermodynamic Principle of Structural Melting**

A structural matrix remains solid if its Gibbs Free Energy of dissociation ($\\Delta G\_{\\text{diss}}$) remains heavily positive ($\\Delta G \> 0$). The structural transition from a solid lattice to a dissolved or liquefied state requires:

$$\\Delta G \= \\Delta H \- T\\Delta S \< 0$$ 

Where:

* $\\Delta H$ is the enthalpy of the molecular bonds holding the tissue solid.

* $T$ is temperature.

* $\\Delta S$ is the entropy of the system.

When sepsis-induced metabolic changes lower the local electron availability, the binding enthalpy ($\\Delta H$) of structural matrices drops to zero. As a result, the entropic driving force ($T\\Delta S$) dominates, causing the organized solid structure to spontaneously "melt" into a high-entropy liquid pool.

**3.1 Ionic Matrix Dissolution (Hard Tissue Lysis)**

Solid human bone relies on the mineral phase hydroxyapatite, $\\text{Ca}\_{10}(\\text{PO}\_4)\_6(\\text{OH})\_2$, stabilized by an intricate network of ionic bonds between divalent calcium cations ($\\text{Ca}^{2+}$) and polyatomic phosphate anions ($\\text{PO}\_4^{3-}$).

During severe sepsis, anaerobic glycolysis creates a massive localized accumulation of protons ($H^+$). This shifts the chemical equilibrium via a series of protonation events that strip electrons away from the ionic lattice interface:

$$\\text{PO}\_4^{3-} \+ H^+ \\rightleftharpoons \\text{HPO}\_4^{2-}$$   
$$\\text{HPO}\_4^{2-} \+ H^+ \\rightleftharpoons \\text{H}\_2\\text{PO}\_4^{-}$$ 

**The Mathematical Collapse:**

The initial electrostatic attractive force ($F\_C$) between the components of the lattice is governed by Coulomb's Law:

$$F\_C \= k\_e \\frac{q\_1 q\_2}{r^2} \= k\_e \\frac{(+2)(-3)}{r^2} \= \-\\frac{6 k\_e}{r^2}$$ 

When the localized proton influx neutralizes the phosphate ion down to a net charge of $-1$ ($\\text{H}\_2\\text{PO}\_4^{-}$), the updated mathematical attraction becomes:

$$F\_{C,\\text{sepsis}} \= k\_e \\frac{(+2)(-1)}{r^2} \= \-\\frac{2 k\_e}{r^2}$$ 

This represents a **66.7% reduction** in the binding force holding the solid crystal together. The lattice can no longer withstand mechanical stress; the ionic bonds break completely, and the solid matrix dissolves into soluble, aqueous ions (bone lysis).

**3.2 Electrostatic and Hydrogen Bond Collapse (Soft Tissue Liquefaction)**

In soft tissues (such as the brain, liver, and subcutaneous fascia), structural stability is maintained by the folding and cross-linking of protein scaffolds like Type I Collagen and extracellular matrix (ECM) adhesion proteins. These structures rely on precise electrostatic attractions between ionized side chains, such as negative carboxylate groups ($\\text{COO}^-$) and positive amino groups ($\\text{NH}\_3^+$).

When sepsis drives down local pH, the massive influx of protons ($H^+$) forces a net charge reorganization via protonation of the negative carboxylate poles:

$$\\text{COO}^- \+ H^+ \\rightarrow \\text{COOH}$$ 

**The Mathematical Collapse:**

The localized charge state transitions from $-1$ to $0$. Consequently, the Coulombic binding force drops to zero:

$$F\_C \= k\_e \\frac{(0)(+1)}{r^2} \= 0$$ 

Without these negative charges to anchor the structural fold, the protein chains repel each other due to remaining unneutralized positive charges. The proteins uncoil, lose their structural alignment, and completely lose their mechanical tensile strength.

**4\. Pathological Translation Across Organ Systems**

The mathematical collapse of these molecular bonds leads directly to the macro-scale transformations observed by clinicians and radiologists during severe sepsis:

1. **Liquefactive Necrosis (Brain and Central Nervous System)**: As electron-sharing frameworks within lipid membranes and neural structural proteins fail ($F\_C \\rightarrow 0$), the solid, highly organized brain parenchyma loses its structural integrity. The cellular structures completely dissolve into an amorphous fluid pool, leaving a permanent lytic cavity.

2. **Cavitary Lung Lesions**: Severe bacterial sepsis in the lungs destroys the alveolar walls. The degradation of cellular adhesion bonds allows the elastic tissue to recoil and fragment, leaving behind hollow, air- or fluid-filled lytic voids within the lung parenchyma.

3. **Dermatolytic/Epidermolytic Tissue Breakdown (Necrotizing Fasciitis)**: Bacterial toxins and metabolic acidosis neutralize the cellular "glue" holding the dermal layers together. Because there are no longer stable electrostatic or ionic configurations to resist shearing forces, the solid tissue sheets literally melt away into a fluid-rich, necrotic sludge.

**5\. Formal Conclusion for Scientific and Regulatory Evaluation**

Every anatomical structure in the human body requires a precise, non-zero distribution of electrons to generate the chemical bonds that keep it solid.

This biochemical report demonstrates that **sepsis is fundamentally a state of localized and systemic charge destabilization**. The high concentration of protons ($H^+$) acts as an electron-depleting sink, neutralizing essential negative charges across the body's tissues.

When these charge values are driven to zero, the binding forces drop to zero ($F\_C \\rightarrow 0$), lowering the thermodynamic activation barrier for structural breakdown. As a direct consequence, hard mineral lattices and soft tissue matrices shift phase from organized solids into unstructured liquids. This physical-chemistry collapse provides a complete mathematical and chemical explanation for the development of severe lytic lesions across all major human organ systems.
